The Weight Rack: Privacy Policy

Last updated: October 21, 2026

Who we are

The Weight Rack is an app that plans and tracks weight-training workouts. Questions about your data: use Feedback in the app, or email the address in the TestFlight invitation.

What we collect

Your email address and a password (the password is handled by our sign-in provider and is never visible to us in readable form). Profile details you choose to enter: name, age, gender, height, weight, phone number, mailing address, your goal, experience level, and training preferences. Workout data: your plans, sets, weights, reps, notes, the gyms and equipment you set up, and the feedback you send us. If you add a professional gym (not a home gym), the street address you type for it (and, so that gyms can be found near you, the map position of that address).

How we use it

To build and adjust your workouts, show your history and stats, and improve the app and its workout engine. We also study the equipment in home gyms as plain anonymous lists, with no name or street address attached (only the city and state you entered, if any), to learn what a typical home gym holds. We do not sell your data and we do not show ads. The app has no third-party analytics or advertising tools.

Where it is stored

Your data is stored with our backend provider, Supabase, which hosts it on our behalf. It is sent over encrypted connections.

On your device

If you turn on Face ID, your sign-in is kept in the iPhone’s secure keychain and unlocked with Face ID. We never receive your face data; it stays with iOS. If you allow Apple Music access, the app only sends play, pause and skip commands.

Photos, location, notifications and Health

Photos: gym staff can photograph equipment, label plates and problems for their gym’s inventory and work orders; these are kept privately and only that gym’s staff can see them. If you print a paper workout sheet and photograph it afterwards, the photo is sent to be read and is not kept. To read a label or a handwritten sheet, the photo is sent to Anthropic (the company behind Claude), which processes it only to read the text for us. Location: only when you tap “Show gyms near me”, the app reads your position once to list nearby gyms; it is not stored or shared. Notifications: if you turn them on in Settings, we keep a token for your phone so a gym’s message or a work order can reach you; turn them off at any time. Apple Health: if you turn it on in Settings, each finished workout is written to Apple Health as a strength-training workout. The app never reads anything from Health.

Community

Community is off until you turn it on. When it is on you can pair with a friend using a short code (whoever enters the other’s code becomes their buddy; either of you can end it at any time). Buddies can see that you finished a workout (its name and how many exercises and sets), your personal records such as an exercise with its weight and reps, how many workouts you did this week, and your place on a weekly leaderboard that shows only you and your buddies. They never see your plans, your body measurements, your other weights or your profile. You can switch off sharing your training, or your place on the leaderboard, separately, and you can turn Community off completely: you then disappear from your buddies’ screens at once. A buddy can send you a high five, which can arrive as a notification if you have notifications on.

Gyms you share

A professional gym is shared by everyone who uses it: its name, the street address typed for it, and its equipment list can be seen, and the equipment list updated, by any user of the app who adds that gym. A home gym is private: it is not listed in search and it stores no address. Someone who has its 6-character gym code can join it, and a member can allow a request to join. To offer that request, the app compares the street address and zip on your profile with the profiles of people who already belong to a home gym. It uses a cleaned-up version of those two fields for the comparison and does not show it to anyone. The member who is asked to approve you sees only your first name.

Gym owners and staff

A gym can have an owner recognised in the app (we approve each one). The owner sees counts for their own gym: how many members it has, how many workouts were logged there this week and this month, and how many times members reported a piece of equipment as not available. If you join a gym that has an owner by entering that gym’s own code, you are asked to share your name and how many workouts you log at that gym with its staff; you can turn this on or off for each gym in Settings → Your Gyms, and it is off when you add a gym any other way. Staff never see which exercises you do, your weights, your plans, or your profile details. The owner can also post a short announcement that members of that gym see on their home screen. A hotel fitness room works the same way for guests (it is a shared gym found by its address), but its operator sees only how many different people used it each month and how its equipment is used compared with other hotel gyms: never names, activity, announcements or any say in the equipment list. Owners can also invite managers and employees to their gym’s staff with a short code; staff see that gym’s equipment inventory and work orders, and managers see the staff list. They never see members’ workouts. Everyone who uses a gym helps keep its equipment list accurate: your taps are counted as votes, weighted by how often you train there, and an owner’s count more, so no single person sets the list alone.

Sharing

We do not share your personal data with other people, apart from the shared gyms and the gym owner and staff sharing described above. A trainer you link to with their code, in Settings → My Trainer, sees your goal, split, method and experience, and the workouts you finish with every exercise, weight and rep (never your body measurements or contact details), and can send you notes; you can stop sharing at any time and their access ends at once. We may disclose data if the law requires it.

Deleting your account

You can delete your account at any time in Settings → Account Settings → Delete My Account. That erases your profile, contact details, plans, preferences, benchmarks and login. Your training history (which exercises were done, and the weights, reps and dates of each set) is kept in anonymous form so the app can learn how to build better workouts for everyone: it is moved to a shared system account, it is no longer linked to your name, email or anything else that identifies you, and any notes you wrote on it are erased. If you set up a home gym, the list of equipment in it is kept the same way, as a plain list with no name or street address, together with the city and state you entered (if any). Feedback you sent us is kept the same way, without your identity. A gym that another member still uses stays with that member; any requests to join a gym that you made or answered are erased with your account.

Children

The app is not intended for children under 13.

Changes

If this policy changes, the date at the top will change.
